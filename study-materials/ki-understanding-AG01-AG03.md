# Understanding KI-AG-01 and KI-AG-03: signals, teaching and items

9 Nov 2026, for Bijit to check before any screen is built. These are the first two knowledge items on her equations route; they lead to SK-AG-30 (solving equations with x on both sides). Both statements are quoted from her graph (`knowledge-graph/graph-data.json`). They are mathematical statements. Bags, counters and balances are not part of either; a picture appears below only where it helps one task.

Child-facing strings are in quotation marks and follow `instruction-language` and `question-formation`. Everything else is for the teacher.

## How "understood" is judged (both items)

From the research read on 9 Nov (sources at the end). Each point is a design choice built on it, not a proven threshold.

1. **Several kinds of task, not one.** Conceptual knowledge is shown by different kinds of task: judging examples, explaining, translating, producing (Rittle-Johnson, Schneider & Star 2015). One right answer to one kind of question is performance, not understanding.
2. **Examples beside near-miss non-examples, then new examples.** A concept is taught with positives and negatives that differ only in the deciding feature, and tested on examples not used in teaching (Engelmann & Carnine, *Theory of Instruction*).
3. **Moving between forms.** Words, symbols, values in a table, and a picture where there is one, saying the same thing (Lesh's translation model; Hiebert & Carpenter, understanding as connections).
4. **Her own examples.** What she makes up shows how far her idea of the concept stretches (Watson & Mason, learner-generated examples).
5. **Judging worked answers, including wrong ones.** Explaining incorrect worked examples helped conceptual understanding in algebra (Booth, Lange, Koedinger & Newton 2013).
6. **The documented misreadings each have an item that would expose them.**
7. **Use without being told, and after a gap.** The item is used inside a later task with nobody saying so, and a fresh set is right after a gap of about 3 days.

**When an item counts as held** (the Student 1 stages, provisional):
1. the worked examples, then one supported item;
2. her own items, until the last one is right **and** every contrast case listed for the item has been right on her own, **and** at least three of the signal kinds (S1, S2, …) have been right on her own;
3. a mixed set with the other item;
4. a fresh set after the gap.

A right answer after a reply counts as helped. Two items in a row not right on her own end that practice for the day. A chosen reason (a two-choice question) never counts on its own; it is always paired with a value or a written answer about the same case.

## How each item is taught (both items)

From a second round of reading on 9 Nov, plus Student 1's `principles-layer.md` (2 Nov), which already grounds most of this. Each line says the source's claim, then what it decides here.

1. **Small steps, each followed by her own step.** Rosenshine: present new material in small steps, with practice after each step. Sweller and colleagues: a worked example is studied reliably only when the learner completes part of it (completion problems). **Here:** every worked example (W1, W2, W3) ends with one step she does herself, never with a Next button alone. The step she completes varies across examples, so that "fill the last box" cannot score.
2. **Vary what does not matter, keep the deciding feature.** Engelmann: positives vary every irrelevant feature; a negative differs from its positive only in the deciding feature; test on new examples. **Here:**
   - the letter varies (x, y, n, a), so "a variable is x" cannot form;
   - the numbers vary, and so does the story (pens, then books, then a bus fare);
   - each near miss sits beside its positive: x + 4 beside 4 + 4, the number of pens beside the pens, 3x + 2 beside 3x + 2 = 11.
   - Yes and No answers do not alternate, and right options are not always in the same place.
3. **The letter is always a number, never an abbreviation.** Küchemann's letter-as-object reading, and the advice against "3a means 3 apples" (the "fruit salad algebra" that teachers' guides warn about). **Here:** a letter is never the first letter of the thing counted (no p for pens). Every story that uses a letter says what number it stands for ("x is the number of pens Asha buys").
4. **Correct examples first, then incorrect ones, each with a short explanation.** Booth and colleagues 2013, and Barbieri & Booth 2020: explaining incorrect examples helped middle-school algebra more than solving problems. Combining two extra supports (signals and written prompts) did not add to either alone, in a study of 202 middle-schoolers. **Here:**
   - the incorrect worked answers (S7, T5) come only after her own items on the same signal have been right;
   - each has one explanation prompt, given as a choice of reasons;
   - each is followed by a value she works out, so the chosen reason is never the only evidence;
   - there is one support at a time.
5. **Feedback after her attempt: the correct answer, the reason, one idea.** Shute 2008: feedback is specific, comes after the learner's own attempt, and is kept short; complex feedback can hurt. Metcalfe (in Student 1's notes): an error helps only when the feedback gives the correct answer. **Here:** every reply says what her answer is, if that can be told, then the right relation in one or two sentences. There is never a bare "not quite", and never the answer before she has tried.
6. **Fade guidance on evidence.** Expertise reversal (Kalyuga and colleagues 2003): guidance that helps a beginner slows down someone who already knows. **Here:** if her completion steps in W1–W3 are all right at the first try, the supported item is skipped and her own items start. This is recorded, so it is visible. *Choice for you: keep this, or always show the supported item.*
7. **Mixed practice, judged later.** Rohrer and colleagues 2020, 54 Grade 7 classes, randomised: the same problems, mixed instead of blocked, scored 61% against 38% on an unannounced test a month later (d = 0.83). Mixed practice took longer. **Here:** once both items hold, they are practised mixed, together with later items. An item counts as held only after the fresh set after the gap, never on the day it was learned.
8. **Review at the start of every session.** Rosenshine: daily review, then weekly and monthly. **Here:** each session opens with one item of every item already held. A wrong one sends that item back to its practice (the Student 1 rule).
9. **High success, but on her own.** Rosenshine's rule of thumb is about 80% right in practice; it is a classroom heuristic, not a finding. Bjork: a difficulty is desirable only if she can meet it. **Here:** no percentage is set. The two-misses stop and the worked example at the next session keep her from repeated failure.
10. **Without an adult.**
    - Every screen has Read it and Pause.
    - Replies are short.
    - A stopped practice is a saved stop, and nothing waits for a teacher.

## KI-AG-01

**Statement (graph):** A variable is a letter that stands for a number that can take different values.
**Kind:** concept. **First grade:** 6. **Gate:** before teaching.

### What understanding looks like

| Signal | She shows it when she… | Misreading it would expose |
|---|---|---|
| S1 Value | works out x + 4, x − 2, 3 + x for a given x | **letter ignored**: answers 4 for x + 4 (Küchemann) |
| S2 Range | says whether an expression can take a value, and for which x, including 0 and a decimal | "a letter is one fixed number", "x + x must be even" |
| S3 Number, not object | says that x is the number of pens, not the pens | **letter as object** (Küchemann; MacGregor & Stacey) |
| S4 Same letter, same number | works out x + x + 2; says two different letters may be equal | each x in x + x a different number; "x and y must differ" |
| S5 Words and symbols | writes "4 less than a number x" as x − 4, and reads x − 4 back in words | order reversed: 4 − x |
| S6 Own example | gives two different values of x that make a statement true | only one fixed value comes to mind |
| S7 Worked answer | judges "5x means 5 pens" and corrects it | letter as object, held as a rule |

### Teaching: worked examples, each set side by side

**W1. A letter stands for a number, and the number can change.**
"In x + 4, the letter x stands for a number."
A small table is the picture here, and it is the only one in this item:

| x | x + 4 |
|---|---|
| 3 | 3 + 4 = 7 |
| 10 | 10 + 4 = 14 |
| 0 | 0 + 4 = 4 |
| 8 | ▢ |

"The number that x stands for can be different each time. When x is 0, x + 4 is 4."
Her step: "When x is 8, what is x + 4?" (12). Then, with a new letter: "In n + 4, the letter n stands for a number too. When n is 8, what is n + 4?" (12).

**W2. x stands for the number of things, not for the things.** Positive and near miss together:
"A pen costs ₹5. Asha buys x pens."
"x stands for the number of pens Asha buys. x does not stand for the pens."
"The cost of the pens in rupees is 5 × x. We write this as 5x."
"If Asha buys 4 pens, x is 4, and the cost is 5 × 4 = ₹20."
Her step: "A book costs ₹30. Ravi buys y books. What does y stand for?" Choices: "the books" / "the number of books Ravi buys" / "the cost of the books". Then "Ravi buys 2 books. What is 30y?" (60).

**W3. The same letter is the same number. Different letters can be different numbers, or the same number.**
"In x + x, both x's stand for the same number. If x is 4, x + x is 4 + 4 = 8."
"x and y can stand for different numbers, like 2 and 7. They can also stand for the same number, like 5 and 5."
Her step: "If a is 6, what is a + a?" (12).

**Supported item, straight after W1–W3:** "If x is 6, what is x + 4?" (answer 10; recorded as supported).

### Her items, by signal

Numbers are drawn fresh for each item; the examples show one draw. Numbers are chosen so that the usual wrong answers differ from each other and from the right answer. The letter changes between items (x, y, n, a), and so does the story.

**S1 Value.**
- "If x is 6, what is x + 4?" Answer 10.
  - 6 → "6 is the number that x stands for. The question asks for x + 4, which is 6 + 4."
  - 4 → "4 is the number added to x. x stands for 6, so x + 4 is 6 + 4."
  - 64 → "x + 4 means the number x, then 4 added. It is 6 + 4, not 6 and 4 written side by side."
- Varied forms: "If x is 9, what is x − 2?" (7); "If x is 5, what is 3 + x?" (8).

**S2 Range** (two parts; both must be right).
- "Can x + 4 be 20? If it can, what number does x stand for?" Buttons "Yes, it can" / "No, it cannot", then x = ▢. Answer yes, 16.
- Contrast cases:
  - "Can x + 4 be 4?" Yes, x is 0. If she says no: "When x is 0, x + 4 is 0 + 4 = 4. x can stand for 0."
  - "Can x + x be 7?" Yes, x is 3.5. If she says no: "When x is 3.5, x + x is 3.5 + 3.5 = 7. x can stand for a number that is not a whole number." (The keypad needs a decimal point.)

**S3 Number, not object** (a choice, paired with a value about the same story).
- "A pen costs ₹5. Asha buys x pens. What does x stand for?"
  - "the pens"
  - "the number of pens Asha buys" (right)
  - "the cost of the pens"
- If she picks "the pens": "x stands for a number. Here it is the number of pens Asha buys."
- If she picks the cost: "The cost is 5x rupees. x is the number of pens."
- Paired value: "Asha buys 3 pens. What is 5x?" Answer 15.
  - 53 → "5x means 5 × x. When x is 3, 5x is 5 × 3."
  - 8 → "5x means 5 × x, not 5 + x."

**S4 Same letter, same number.**
- "If x is 3, what is x + x + 2?" Answer 8.
  - 5 → "There are two x's. Each one stands for 3. 3 + 3 + 2."
- "In x + x, can the first x be 2 and the second x be 5?" No. If she says yes: "In one expression, every x stands for the same number."
- "Can x and y stand for the same number?" Yes. If she says no: "Different letters can stand for the same number. x can be 5 and y can be 5."

**S5 Words and symbols** (keypad with x, +, −).
- "Write 4 more than a number x as an expression." Answer x + 4 (4 + x also accepted).
- "Write 4 less than a number x as an expression." Answer x − 4.
  - 4 − x → "4 − x is x less than 4. In 4 less than a number x, the number x comes first, then 4 is taken away. That is x − 4."
- "Write 3 times a number x as an expression." Answer 3x (3 × x accepted, with "We write this as 3x.").
- The other way: "What does x − 4 say in words?"
  - "4 less than a number x" (right)
  - "a number x less than 4"
  - "4 more than a number x"

**S6 Own example.**
- "Give a number for x so that x + 2 is more than 10." Any number greater than 8.
  - 8 → "When x is 8, x + 2 is 10. 10 is not more than 10."
- Then "Give a different number that also works." Any other number greater than 8.

**S7 Worked answer** (incorrect example, then a correction).
- "A pen costs ₹5. Asha buys x pens. Ravi says that 5x means 5 pens. Is Ravi right?" Buttons "Yes, Ravi is right" / "No, Ravi is not right".
- Then "What does 5x stand for?"
  - "the cost of the pens in rupees" (right)
  - "5 pens"
  - "the number of pens"

**Contrast cases for the held rule:** x = 0 (S2), a decimal value (S2), the order in x − 4 (S5), the same letter twice (S4), number not object (S3 or S7).

**Used later without being told:** every KI-AG-03 item uses KI-AG-01. A wrong value there that ignores the letter or treats it as an object is recorded against KI-AG-01 as well.

## KI-AG-03

**Statement (graph):** An algebraic expression is a combination of numbers and variables joined by operation signs, and it contains no equals sign.
**Kind:** concept. **First grade:** 6. **Gate:** before teaching. Uses KI-AG-01.

### What understanding looks like

| Signal | She shows it when she… | Misreading it would expose |
|---|---|---|
| T1 Sort | tells expressions from equations and from things that are neither | "an expression must have an answer, so it needs =" (the operational reading of =; Knuth et al. 2006) |
| T2 Structure | says what is done to x in 3x + 2, and works out its value | 3x read as a two-digit number; 3x read as 3 + x; 3(x + 2) |
| T3 Same and different | sees that 3x + 2 and 2 + 3x always have the same value, and 2x + 3 does not | expressions with the same numbers are the same |
| T4 Words to symbols | writes "2 more than 3 times a number x" | order of operations lost |
| T5 Worked answer | rejects 3x + 2 = 5x and shows why with a value | **conjoining**: 3x + 2 written as 5x (Subramaniam 2018 lists 5 + 2x = 7x) |
| T6 Own example | writes an expression with x that is 11 when x is 3 | only copies a shown expression |

### Teaching

**W1. What an expression is, beside near misses.**

| Written | Expression? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 3x + 2 | yes | numbers and a letter joined by + |
| x | yes | a letter alone |
| 7 | yes | a number alone |
| 3x + 2 = 11 | no | it has an equals sign; it is an equation |
| 3x + | no | the + joins nothing on the right |

"An expression is made of numbers and letters joined by +, −, × or ÷. An expression has no equals sign."
Her step: "Is 2y + 1 = 7 an expression?" (no), then "Is 2y + 1 an expression?" (yes).

**W2. What 3x + 2 says.**
"3x + 2 tells you to take the number x, multiply it by 3, then add 2."
"If x is 5, 3x + 2 is 3 × 5 + 2 = 15 + 2 = 17."
Her step: "If n is 6, what is 2n + 1?" (13).

**W3. Same value every time, or not.**

| x | 3x + 2 | 2 + 3x | 2x + 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| 4 | 14 | 14 | 11 |

"3x + 2 and 2 + 3x have the same value for every x. 2x + 3 gives 5 when x is 1, but 11 when x is 4, so it is a different expression."
Her step: "When x is 6, what is 3x + 2? What is 2x + 3?" (20 and 15).

**Supported item:** "If x is 4, what is 3x + 2?" (answer 14).

### Her items, by signal

**T1 Sort** (one at a time, Yes/No; several items so a pattern of answers cannot score).
- "Is 2x − 5 an expression?" (yes)
- "Is 2x − 5 = 9 an expression?" (no)
  - If she says yes: "2x − 5 = 9 has an equals sign. It says two things have the same value, so it is an equation."
- "Is y an expression?" (yes)
- "Is 4 × x an expression?" (yes)
- "Is x − an expression?" (no)
  - If she says yes: "The − in x − takes away nothing. An expression joins numbers and letters on both sides of each sign."
- Every fourth sort item pairs with "Why is it not an expression?" (two choices), counted only with the sort answer beside it.

**T2 Structure** (value, with x drawn so the wrong answers differ).
- "If x is 5, what is 3x + 2?" Answer 17.
  - 37 → "3x means 3 × x. When x is 5, 3x is 3 × 5 = 15, not 35."
  - 10 → "3x means 3 × x, not 3 + x."
  - 21 → "21 is 3 × (5 + 2). In 3x + 2, x is multiplied by 3 first, then 2 is added."
- "In 4x − 1, what is done to the number x?"
  - "multiply it by 4, then take away 1" (right)
  - "take away 1, then multiply by 4"
  - "add 4, then take away 1"

**T3 Same and different** (values first, then the judgement).
- "If x is 2, what is 5x + 1? What is 1 + 5x?" Both 11.
- Then "Do 5x + 1 and 1 + 5x have the same value for every x?" Yes.
- Contrast: "If x is 2, what is 5x + 1? What is x + 5?" (11 and 7). Then "Are 5x + 1 and x + 5 the same expression?" No.

**T4 Words to symbols.**
- "Write 2 more than 3 times a number x as an expression." Answer 3x + 2 (2 + 3x accepted).
  - 3 + 2x → "3 + 2x is 3 more than 2 times x. Here x is multiplied by 3, then 2 is added."
- "Write 5 less than 2 times a number x as an expression." Answer 2x − 5.
  - 5 − 2x → "5 − 2x takes 2x away from 5. Here 5 is taken away from 2x."

**T5 Worked answer** (incorrect example).
- "Meera writes 3x + 2 = 5x. Is Meera right?" Buttons "Yes, Meera is right" / "No, Meera is not right".
- Then the check, typed: "If x is 2, what is 3x + 2? What is 5x?" (8 and 10).
- Reply: "When x is 2, 3x + 2 is 8 and 5x is 10. They are not equal, so 3x + 2 is not 5x. 3x and 2 cannot be joined into one term."
- x is never 1 here, because at x = 1 both are 5, which would look like support for Meera.

**T6 Own example.**
- "Write an expression with x in it that is 11 when x is 3." Any valid expression with x whose value at 3 is 11 (3x + 2, x + 8, 4x − 1, …).
- The reply works it out at x = 3: "When x is 3, 3x + 2 is 3 × 3 + 2 = 11. Yes."
- If her expression has no x: "11 is a number with no x in it. Write one that uses x."

**Contrast cases for the held rule:**
- an equation beside its expression (T1);
- an incomplete form (T1);
- 3x as 3 × x, not 35 or 3 + x (T2);
- the same numbers in a different order giving a different expression (T3);
- conjoining (T5).

**Used later without being told:** KI-AG-04 and KI-AG-06 (terms and coefficients) and every equation item. A conjoined answer there (for example 2x + 3 = 5x inside a later step) is recorded against KI-AG-03.

## What is not decided

1. **Pictures.** Only the table of values in KI-AG-01 W1 and KI-AG-03 W3 is used. No bags, counters or balance here. Whether a picture is needed at all for the later items (KI-AG-18 onwards) is decided item by item, with the same test: it must carry the mathematics of that item.
2. **Contexts.** Pens and rupees are the only story (S3, S7). Is that a context she knows, or should it come from her textbook?
3. **"x − 4" before negatives.** Every x − 4 item keeps x larger than 4, so the value stays positive.
4. **The keypad** needs x, +, −, a decimal point and digits. Expressions are read back with spaces ("x − 4").
5. **Graph gap** (from the path): KI-AG-20 should be linked to SK-AG-30. This does not affect these two items.
