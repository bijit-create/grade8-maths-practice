# KI-GE-66

> Every quadrilateral has at least one diagonal that lies inside it apart from its end points, and that diagonal divides the quadrilateral into two triangles.

Kind: Principle. Where required, it is required **before attainment**. Without rationale. First expected in Grade 8.

**Note.** authored by ConveGenius, 24 Oct 2026. Two readings of kind: principle, or concept because the result is always two triangles; recorded as a principle. Corrected 11 Nov 2026: the earlier statement did not specify which diagonal. A concave quadrilateral has one interior diagonal that divides it into two triangles; its other diagonal lies outside, apart from its end points. In a convex quadrilateral, either diagonal divides it into two triangles.
