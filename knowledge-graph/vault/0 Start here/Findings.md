## Number and number theory

- Reading used for M803 ("Proves divisibility rules of 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9 and 11"): the task is giving the justification for a stated rule, in words or letters, for a number of two or three digits, from the generalised form 10a + b or 100a + 10b + c. Five justification skills are grouped by the form of the argument: the units digit (rules of 2 and 5, SK-NN-06), the last two digits (rule of 4, SK-NN-07), the digit sum (rules of 3 and 9, SK-NN-08), the alternating sum (rule of 11, SK-NN-09), and the combination of two rules (rule of 6 from the divisibility by 2 and by 3, SK-NN-10). Writing the number in generalised form is the one step (SK-NN-11), always part of SK-NN-06 to 09 because the tasks give the digits and not the form. The SLOF note says the chapter (Playing with Numbers) was removed from the Class 8 textbook in the 2023 rationalisation; the scope here is taken from the earlier Class 8 chapter and the Class 6 chapter that states the tests.
- Left out of M803: applying a rule to decide whether a given number is divisible (the outcome says proves, and no justification needs it); puzzles in which letters stand for digits and the "reversing the digits" problems with 10b + a; the rules for 8 and 10, which the chapter teaches but the outcome does not list; justifications for numbers of four or more digits.
- Reading used for M804 ("Finds squares, cubes and square roots and cube roots of numbers using different methods"): the method is a condition, so methods that give different task types are separate skills. Squares and cubes are found by multiplication (SK-NN-13 and the copied SK-NN-02). Square roots have three methods, each a skill: prime factorisation (SK-NN-18), repeated subtraction of odd numbers (SK-NN-19) and the division method, which has three task types (perfect squares SK-NN-20, decimals SK-NN-21, whole numbers that are not perfect squares to one decimal place SK-NN-22). Cube roots have one method in the chapter, prime factorisation (SK-NN-23). The estimate of a root to one decimal place is read as the end of the division method (SK-NN-22).
- Judgment call in M804: deciding whether a number is a perfect square or cube (SK-NN-14, SK-NN-15) and finding the smallest multiplier or divisor that makes one (SK-NN-16, SK-NN-17) are written as outcome tasks because the scope guidance lists them, but the outcome asks for finding squares, cubes and roots; they trace to the outcome only through the phrase "using different methods" and through the chapter tasks that take a root of the product after finding the multiplier. A reviewer who reads the outcome strictly can remove these four skills and their knowledge items (KI-NN-27, 28, 36, 37).
- Left out of M804: squares and cubes of decimals, fractions and numbers of more than three digits; squares by the identity (a + b)² (algebraic identities belong to another outcome); properties and patterns of squares and cubes (units digits, number of zeros, number of digits in a root, numbers between consecutive squares, triangular numbers, sums of odd numbers as a pattern, 1729 and sums of two cubes); Pythagorean triplets (the outcome asks for finding squares and roots, and the triplet rule m² − 1, 2m, m² + 1 is a property of squares used for a different task); square roots of fractions and cube roots of negative numbers, decimals and fractions; the least number to be added to or subtracted from a number to make a perfect square; word problems that use squares and roots, such as the side of a square park or the rows of an arrangement.
- Reading used for M805 ("Solves problems with integral exponents"): the outcome tasks are problems and simplifications that use the laws, so bare evaluation of one power is a step (SK-NN-24), always part of SK-NN-27 and sometimes part of SK-NN-25, SK-NN-26 and SK-NN-30 (in SK-NN-30 only when the exponent is negative). The outcome tasks are: simplifying powers of one base (SK-NN-25), simplifying powers of different bases with the same exponent (SK-NN-26), evaluating an expression with negative exponents (SK-NN-27), finding an unknown exponent (SK-NN-28), writing a number in standard form and in usual form (SK-NN-29, SK-NN-30), comparing numbers in standard form (SK-NN-31) and finding how many times one quantity is as large as another (SK-NN-32). Standard form and comparison are included because the scope guidance places them in this outcome; the outcome text does not mention them. Recall of the laws is recorded as knowledge items (KI-NN-40 to 49).
- Left out of M805: adding and subtracting numbers in standard form, such as the total mass of the Earth and the Moon (the textbook has it, the scope guidance does not list it; add it as a skill if the reviewer wants it); fractional exponents; powers with letters as bases, as in 25 × t^(−4) ÷ (5^(−3) × 10 × t^(−8)), because they belong with algebraic expressions; use of a calculator.

## Algebraic generalisation

- Counts: 17 new skills (12 outcome tasks: SK-AG-25 to 28, 33 to 36 and 38 to 41; 5 steps: SK-AG-29 to 32 and 37), 14 new and 35 copied knowledge items, no skill copied. Review stays pending.
- M806, reading used: 'using variables' is read as writing and solving a linear equation in one variable for a problem, because NCERT Class 8 Chapter 2 (Linear Equations in One Variable) is the only Grade 8 chapter that sets puzzles and daily-life problems with a variable. 'Puzzles' and 'daily life problems' are not two skills, since both are written and solved the same way; the situation is a condition of SK-AG-25 (numbers, consecutive numbers, ages, ratio, perimeter, money). Digit puzzles (SK-AG-26), equations with the variable on both sides (SK-AG-27) and equations with fractions or the variable in a denominator (SK-AG-28) each get an outcome task because the chapter gives each its own worked examples and a learner can pass one and fail another.

## Geometry

- Counts (authored by ConveGenius, 24 Oct 2026): 25 new skills, SK-GE-08 to SK-GE-32 (22 outcome tasks, 3 steps) for LO-M811 to LO-M815; no skill is copied. 56 new knowledge items, KI-GE-21 to KI-GE-76, and 14 knowledge items copied. Every new skill has review pending.
- LO-M811, reading used (authored by ConveGenius): the outcome says "problems related to angles of a quadrilateral using angle sum property". Read as finding unknown angles of a convex quadrilateral by the angle sum of 360°, in three task types: the fourth angle from three angles in degrees (SK-GE-08), angles given as expressions in x (SK-GE-09) and angles given in a ratio (SK-GE-10). The three differ in what the learner must do before the angle sum is used (read off, write and solve an equation, share 360° in a ratio), and the NCERT Class 8 exercises on quadrilaterals include all three. SK-GE-09 and SK-GE-10 are linked as one condition in two forms ("is format of") but kept as two skills, because a learner can solve one and not the other.
- LO-M811, step: SK-GE-11 finds an interior angle from an exterior angle at the same vertex, because NCERT figures give one angle of the quadrilateral as an angle on a straight line with a side extended. It is a step that is sometimes part of SK-GE-08. It is not absorbed (see below). It uses KI-GE-11 (copied) and KI-GE-24.
- LO-M811, left out because the outcome does not ask for them: the angle sum of polygons with more than four sides, the sum of the exterior angles of a polygon, deriving or verifying the angle sum property of a quadrilateral (splitting it into two triangles is the reason inside KI-GE-23, with rationale, not a task), angles of non-convex quadrilaterals, deciding whether four given angles can be the angles of a quadrilateral, and problems that need a property of a kite or trapezium besides the angle sum.
- LO-M812, reading used (authored by ConveGenius): "verifies properties of parallelograms" is read as two task types, verifying by measuring on one drawn figure (SK-GE-12) and verifying by reasoning for every parallelogram (SK-GE-13 for the angle properties from parallel lines, SK-GE-14 for the side and diagonal properties from congruent triangles). "Properties of parallelograms" is read to include the diagonal property of a rhombus and of a rectangle, which are verified by measuring only; their reasons are not part of the intended learning (rationale without). "Establishes the relationship between them through reasoning" is read as the relationships among parallelogram, rhombus, rectangle and square: which is a special case of which and why (SK-GE-17), and which of the four kinds have a stated property (SK-GE-18). The reason in each case comes from the definitions (KI-GE items KI-GE-31, KI-GE-32, KI-GE-33) and from KI-GE-36, KI-GE-37, KI-GE-38 and KI-GE-39.
- LO-M812, judgment call for the reviewer: SK-GE-15 and SK-GE-16 (finding an unknown angle, side or diagonal segment from the properties) are applications of the properties, not verifications, and the outcome says "verifies". They are kept because the Class 8 exercises on parallelograms are almost wholly of this kind and the reason for each step is a property, so they show the properties used through reasoning. If the reviewer reads the outcome strictly, these two skills can be removed; the property items stay, because SK-GE-18 and the constructions (SK-GE-31) use them.
- LO-M812, left out because the outcome does not ask for them: the converses (deciding whether a quadrilateral with equal opposite sides, or with diagonals that bisect each other, is a parallelogram), properties of the kite and the trapezium, reasons for the diagonal properties of the rhombus and the rectangle, and NCERT problems that give angles of a parallelogram as a ratio or as expressions in x.

Four task types: the three views of a solid made of cubes (SK-GE-19), the three views of a simple solid or of an object made of two simple solids (SK-GE-20), an oblique sketch of a cuboid or a cube (SK-GE-21) and an isometric sketch of a cuboid or a cube (SK-GE-22).
